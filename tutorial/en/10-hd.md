# HD

## Two pictures per frame

An ordinary project keeps two colored pictures of a frame: the 128x32 one (SD) and, if you make
it, a 256x64 one (HD) with four times the pixels. A display or a virtual pinball setup that can
show 256x64 shows the HD picture; everything else shows SD. A frame without HD is upscaled by
libserum.

`SD` and `HD` in the toolbar (or `Ctrl/Cmd+X`) choose which picture you paint. The original frame
below stays 128x32.

## Making HD

1. Colorize the frame in SD first.
2. Select the frames and press `Create HD` in the `Frame` section. The SD picture is scaled to
   twice its size.
3. The window asks how: `Nearest` doubles every pixel, `Scale2x` keeps the colors and smooths
   diagonal edges, `Bilinear`, `Bicubic` and `Lanczos4` blend. It also offers to upscale the
   frames' background masks and backgrounds.
4. Switch to `HD` and refine: rounder curves, finer gradients, details SD has no room for.

`Remove HD` takes the HD picture away again. Backgrounds and sprites have `Create HD` of their
own.

## Images and movie clips

An HD picture can also come from outside: a drawing, a rendered image, a movie clip over many
frames. Select the frames (and, on the canvas, the pixels that get the picture), switch to `HD`,
and use `File -> Import Image Into Selected Frames...` (or `Import Image` in the `Frame`
section) or `File -> Import Movie Clip Into Selected Frames...`. The window that opens chooses
the part of the picture that goes into the selection, and can move it across the frames.

## What stays SD

Dynamic zones are defined on the 128x32 picture. libserum colors them there and upscales the
result into the HD picture, so a score keeps working on HD frames without zones of its own. In
HD mode the `Dynamic` button is off.

For pixel art colored through a dynamic set on a frame whose HD picture is an imported image,
`Only on the SD Picture` in the `Dynamic Sets` section keeps the set's zones from covering the
imported picture.

Handbook: "SD And HD Modes", "Saving" ("Importing images and movie clips"), "Dynamic Masks".
